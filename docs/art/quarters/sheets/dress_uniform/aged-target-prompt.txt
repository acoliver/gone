The exact same military dress tunic on the same hanger, same camera, same cut, collar, shoulder boards, brass buttons and medal, but after one thousand years hanging in a sealed, dry, sterile starship locker with no mould, no moths, no water and no plants. The navy wool has faded unevenly to a dusty grey-blue and gone brittle. One shoulder seam has split along the hanger line. One armhole seam has torn open so the empty sleeve hangs partly away from the body, still attached by ragged frayed fabric, pressed flat. The hem is ragged and frayed with a few torn-away strips. A few small worn-through patches along folds. The brass is dull brown with patina, the insignia tarnished but still readable. A thin layer of grey dust lies on the shoulders. A small soft heap of grey-brown fibre dust and a few fallen fabric strips lies below. Plain neutral studio lighting, plain background.
