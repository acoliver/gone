# Gone — Society, History, and Command Traditions

## Purpose and authority

This is a handoff for an agent continuing the worldbuilding for **Gone**. It consolidates the author's decisions from the discussion, including later corrections that supersede earlier assistant interpretations.

- Sections 1–10 record established author direction. Explanatory connections are kept close to what the author supplied.
- Section 11 separates unaccepted assistant proposals and open decisions from established material.
- Earth terms are development shorthand. They are not automatically in-world names, institutions, or exact historical equivalents.
- Do not turn this outline into a conventional European monarchy, a modern nation-state from its beginning, or a story in which industrialization precedes and causes the massacre.
- No calendar dates, durations, population totals, final rank names, or names for the major historical figures have been fixed here.

## 1. Environment and the material basis of society

The planet remained dangerous for much longer than Earth during the development of organized society. Volcanism, destructive waves, severe weather, and other disasters repeatedly decimated populations. Mortality remained high, and continuity required redundancy in essential knowledge and leadership.

The society that became the imperial center occupied the largest stable zone. It also possessed an unusually dependable coast, described by the author as free of the recurring tidal waves and hurricanes that made other places dangerous. This allowed resources, infrastructure, institutions, and expertise to accumulate more consistently.

Volcanism also produced many islands. Chains of stopping places made maritime travel less treacherous than comparable uninterrupted voyages on Earth, or at least attractive relative to the dangers people already faced at home. Expansion could proceed through intermediate stops rather than demanding single enormous crossings.

Securing dependable farmland, harbors, resources, and supply routes was worth substantial risk. Material incentives reinforced the ideological ambition to spread a supposedly perfect society.

Later, geological conditions improved: volcanism abated, destructive waves ceased being a regular occurrence, and usable farmland increased. The weather remained maddening even as geological hazards became less severe. The physical mechanism and exact geological timescale remain unspecified.

## 2. Early development, labor, medicine, and gender

Development did not follow Earth's sequence or timetable. Mixed hunting, gathering, and agriculture transitioned comparatively quickly into complex imperial societies. Technologies advanced early in areas where recurring mortality created strong incentives.

Steam machinery arrived **at the beginning of the empire**, not shortly before the massacre. A small population that was repeatedly decimated increased the appeal of machinery and labor substitution. The family and tribal system averted formal slavery and reinforced the desire to mechanize.

Obstetrics and medicine also developed early. By the late agricultural period, reliable birth control existed, and childbirth deaths were lower than deaths from major environmental hazards. Do not invent a particular drug, biological mechanism, or medical discovery as settled fact; those details have not been chosen.

Sexism existed but was less pronounced than in many Earth analogues. The author used Europe or the US in the 1960s as a rough social comparison for the imperial era: women were integrated into economic work even though significant barriers remained. This is not a claim of identical technology or identical restrictions.

Women's military integration developed later:

1. The expanding empire became stretched thin.
2. Women entered supporting military occupations.
3. Necessity expanded participation into combat.
4. Command integration took longer.
5. Fathers without sons preferred appointing qualified daughters as their command partners, helping open that route.

Do not recast the first powerful fathers doing this as necessarily motivated by general egalitarian principles.

## 3. Families, tribes, and alliances

Family is a durable institution with economic, social, and relational obligations. Its later separation from state authority does not abolish it.

Tribes began principally as **geographic alliances of families**. They are not castes. Class associations exist but are informal. Tribes can form alliances with other tribes, but these are looser than the commitments constituting the tribe itself.

The distinction is important:

| Change | Normal interpretation |
|---|---|
| Families alter the composition of an established tribe | Betrayal of an enduring community |
| A tribe changes its external alliance | The abandoned ally may call it betrayal; others generally see political maneuvering |

Families and tribes can migrate. The future reformer's powerful family and tribe had moved to the eastern continent, although the empire originated elsewhere. Geographic origins do not mean membership automatically disappears upon relocation.

In the later society, tribal identities remain but are less openly political. They can resemble regional, national, or sports loyalties in everyday expression. Relationships still produce preferences and influence in politics and business. This persistence must coexist with individual citizenship and democracy.

## 4. Household relationships, sexuality, and parenthood

There is no formal institution exactly equivalent to marriage, although arrangements functionally resembling it are common. Property and other commitments can be jointly held by agreement.

Family alliances are often strengthened through arranged partnerships or household agreements. The author calls these arranged marriages as shorthand. They remain common but are becoming less fashionable among upper-class families and families whose power is already secure. They still occur there.

Polyamorous families exist and are generally associated more with the lower economic classes. Their legal standing is equivalent; the society does not establish a separate inferior legal category for them.

Homosexuality is not especially taboo. Failing to have children attracts more disapproval. The expectation is approximately: being gay is acceptable, but produce children with someone.

### Fours

- A common arrangement consists of a lesbian couple and a gay male couple forming a four-person family.
- Fours exist across all classes. Their prevalence is distinct from the class association of broader polyamorous households.
- Do not assume all four members desire or have sex with every other member.
- The term or gesture for **four** later acquires a broader meaning referring to a gay or lesbian person.
- It is not intrinsically derogatory but can be used that way.
- Example social use: someone expresses attraction to a person, and a friend signals four to indicate that the person is gay or otherwise unavailable to that approach.

Before women could serve, male same-sex relationships were more common in the military. Participants did not necessarily regard themselves as constituting a couple in the relevant household or social sense. The author's Greek comparison is loose and does not establish identical age structures, practices, or romantic ideals. In particular, do not romanticize it as a culture necessarily celebrating poetry about male lovers.

Detailed rules for parenthood, adoption, infertility, inheritance, household departure, and relationship exclusivity remain open.

## 5. Early eastern contact and communications

Regular contact with the eastern continent existed relatively early. Eastern societies were not far behind in general advancement. They failed to develop steam and communications in time to prevent the emerging empire's advantage; this is not a story of an advanced civilization encountering a primitive or isolated continent.

Both steam machinery and telegraphic communications belong to the **start of the empire**. Their combination helped make global conquest and administration possible.

The person who invented the telegraph and its voice membrane was an **immigrant from the east**. He was motivated by the desire to communicate with relatives back home. Imperial accounts glossed over this eastern origin and motivation. Do not assume he was entirely erased from official history; selective presentation is the established point.

The system had two coexisting modes:

| Mode | Established purpose |
|---|---|
| Membrane voice transmission | More effective immediate conversation and spoken communication |
| Telegraph code, loosely comparable to Morse | Messages requiring accuracy |

The membrane did not eliminate coded transmission. Precise material continued to be sent in code. No exact engineering mechanism, transmission medium, intercontinental cable route, bandwidth, or signaling alphabet is specified by this handoff.

The inventor's personal motivation belongs naturally in a relationship-centered society. Its later imperial presentation also illustrates how outside contributions could be absorbed into a narrative of the center's excellence.

## 6. The imperial political order

There were **never kings** in this account.

The polity developed from a negotiated order among tribes, and within tribes among powerful families. The author uses **republic** as shorthand while emphasizing that it is not a perfect description and is not what the people necessarily call it.

Initially, politics was heavily based on family alliances, negotiated standing, and arrangements among groups rather than modern nation-state institutions. The author's Habsburg comparison refers to alliance-building through families and relationships; it does not import hereditary monarchy into the setting.

Expansion took several forms:

- Violent conquest.
- Family and household alliances.
- Negotiated incorporation.
- Willing accession by other societies.

Other cultures and societies existed but were increasingly subsumed. **Romanization** is the author's shorthand for forceful assimilation into the imperial order. It can include the loss or suppression of independent cultural and political identities. Do not mechanically import a particular Roman legal or cultural system.

Over time, power became concentrated in **one family**. Its own tribe resented the concentration but tolerated it because membership still made them more powerful than outsiders. Allied tribes had stronger grievances about their subordinate position.

### Imperial ideology

The ruling society was presented as nearly perfect. Spreading it was a duty, and leading families—including less powerful ruling families—were understood to possess a right and responsibility to govern it.

Conquest increased and maintained the dominant tribe's power while also being justified as extending this superior order. Material advantage, family ambition, and sincere belief could coexist.

The author's earlier use of fascism describes features of domination and expansion; it does **not** establish Earth's fascist ideology, its exact institutions, or its historical sequence.

The empire lasted partly because its stable core had durable geographic advantages, and because early machinery, communications, and maritime routes made its reach sustainable.

## 7. Consolidation, stagnation, and the reformer's movement

The world was consolidated **before the massacre**. The overarching state survived the later violence.

After consolidation, the imperial ideology began to break apart. Assimilation had increasingly removed the distinction between the ruling society and external others. Meanwhile, growing economic problems and stagnation supplied grievances.

A powerful figure emerged whom the author provisionally calls **their Atatürk**. This is a conversational reference only. His actual name is undecided, and his biography must not be copied from Earth's Atatürk.

His influential family and tribe had moved to the eastern continent. They had eastern allies and formed further alliances with formerly conquered tribes and other eastern groups.

### His specific historical claim

He praised the ruling family's **ancestors**, their tribe, and the empire those ancestors helped create. He treated imperial expansion as the successful spreading of a nearly perfect social order.

His accusation was that **later generations** became increasingly inbred and corrupt, betrayed ancestral achievements, and captured or distorted the old negotiated order.

Do not rewrite his position as any of the following:

- The ruling family was evil from its beginning.
- The empire's creation was inherently illegitimate in his own ideology.
- He wanted to liberate the world from the founders' supposedly perfect society.
- He initially advocated universal individual democracy.

He invoked an idealized, self-serving ancestral order and called for taking it back from its degenerate present holders. His movement was a power grab presented as necessary restoration and reform.

### Coalition formation and failed repression

His family and tribe broke with the ruling family first. Their power and influence encouraged other alliances to break or change.

The ruling family tried pressure and economic embargo. These measures failed to impose decisive consequences:

- Constant external war had ended, reducing the perceived need for large standing armies.
- Internal security forces were fragmented.
- Other families and tribes were unwilling to cooperate reliably in suppression.
- The eastern coalition possessed its own relationships and support.

Do not assume either that the government had no military capacity or that rapid communications guaranteed obedience.

## 8. Great Reclaiming, massacre, and historical memory

The movement and its violence were celebrated as the **Great Reclaiming**. Later understanding recast the event as the **Great Holocaust** or massacre. These English labels are working terms; precise in-world names and official usage remain unsettled.

The initial declared aim was to eliminate the corrupt ruling family and its allies. The violence extended beyond a single family to allies, collaborators, and associated people. The author describes it as a holocaust and invokes the targeting of inherited or affiliated groups as a comparison.

The existing state survived and was subsequently reformed. This was not the destruction of all planetary government followed by an unrelated replacement civilization.

The reformer was regarded for a long time as virtually messianic. Later society recognizes that he was even more brutal than the ruling family and brought about mass atrocity.

The broader reassessment recognizes continuity in the **violence of the perfect society**: the imperial order imposed its allegedly exemplary society on others; the restorer sought to recover that society by eliminating people blamed for its corruption.

The exact killing mechanisms, death toll, duration, persecution categories, survival of individual families, and stages of historical reassessment have not been established. Do not invent them as settled history.

## 9. Great Pax and rapid industrialization

### Chronology that must be preserved

1. Steam and communications appear at the empire's beginning.
2. Expansion and world consolidation occur across the imperial period.
3. Economic stagnation and political concentration help enable the eastern reformer's coalition.
4. The Great Reclaiming/massacre occurs as more intensive, standardized, large-scale industrialization is beginning.
5. The surviving state is reformed initially toward an idealized pre-imperial negotiated order.
6. The **Great Pax** follows, with rapid industrial development and increasingly rapid social change.
7. Individual democracy develops further while family institutions remain formally intact.

The author compares the pre-massacre material setting roughly to **1700s Britain, but earlier in this civilization's development**, and the emerging intensive production to **Henry Ford-style industrialization**. These analogies identify different aspects of their uneven development; do not use them to assign Earth dates or require Earth's technological chronology.

The rapid industrial acceleration follows from a combination of:

- Post-massacre political stability.
- An existing world government.
- Machinery and communications already available.
- A relatively small population, historically subject to repeated decimation.
- Family and tribal institutions that had prevented formal slavery.
- Increasingly stable planetary conditions.
- Expanding usable farmland.

Volcanism was abating and tidal waves were no longer regular, but difficult weather persisted.

Do not credit prosperity solely to the reformer or treat prosperity as justification for the massacre. The improvement combines political, environmental, and technological developments.

## 10. Democratization and continuing family institutions

The initial democratic element was **negotiation among families and tribes**. Reformers sought to redistribute standing and offices within that framework.

The restored arrangement proved insufficient as industrialization changed social and economic life. People remained expected to help their families, but making public office a family possession became less appealing and less workable.

Representation developed from negotiated allocation of offices toward families voting for representatives, and eventually toward individual voting and increasingly democratic government. Exact reforms and their sequence beyond this broad development remain open.

The endpoint retains the family system formally while **separating it from the state**. Family agreements, care, property, and social obligations can remain important without entitling a family to exercise a member's political rights.

Tribal and family alliances persist informally in politics and business. Modern society is neither free of patronage nor legally organized as an unchanged set of hereditary family offices.

## 11. Dual command and ranks: established direction versus proposals

### Established direction

- Commander, captain, and similar Earth terms have been placeholders.
- Leadership traditions developed around redundancy under sustained high mortality.
- Byzantine duality is an inspiration, not a claim of identical Byzantine institutions.
- Family serving together is culturally less unusual than in many modern Earth forces.
- A dual was frequently a relative expected to cooperate and listen; a daughter is a salient example.
- Wives/romantic household partners were possible duals but generally considered a bad idea.
- Daughter appointments helped women's eventual access to command.
- The hierarchy must fit a history of negotiated family and tribal authority, followed by the separation of family institutions from public power.

### Assistant proposals not yet accepted

Earlier assistant responses suggested separating personal grade, professional qualification, and appointed office; giving one holder operational precedence within a substantial shared appointment; specifying succession and disagreement procedures; and requiring independent qualification for modern family command pairs.

These are useful design candidates, **not established canon**.

The proposed English ranks **Learner, Member, Proven Member, Guide, Keeper, Senior Keeper, and High Keeper** were not accepted. Nor were the suggested appointments and use of First/Second finalized. Do not silently install them in code, dialogue, or lore as authoritative terminology.

The captain's daughter has **not** been established by this discussion as his dual, executive officer, or a command holder. A family tradition does not determine her individual role.

### Decisions still needed

1. How equal are the holders of a dual appointment, and what distinguishes the dual from an ordinary deputy?
2. Which offices require or permit dual holders?
3. How are disagreement, incapacity, succession, and removal handled?
4. How did appointments change when families lost formal state authority?
5. Which qualifications, career grades, and offices exist in the modern service?
6. What are their English translations and eventual Naleya terms?
7. What actual position, if any, does the captain's daughter hold?

Do not invent Naleya words or modify an existing language system from this outline alone. Use the project's current language references when that work is requested.

## 12. Instructions for the receiving agent

- Preserve the chronology and the author's later corrections above.
- Keep author-established facts distinct from interpretations, design suggestions, and unanswered questions.
- Use Earth research for inspiration and plausibility checks, not as a template that overrides the setting.
- Retain technological unevenness: early steam and voice telegraphy do not imply simultaneous Earth-equivalent development in every field.
- Retain comparable eastern advancement, early regular contact, and the eastern immigrant's invention and family motivation.
- Retain the difference between changing a tribe's membership and changing alliances between tribes.
- Retain a negotiated political order with no kings; republic and empire remain explanatory shorthand.
- Retain the reformer's praise for the founders and his accusation against their later descendants.
- Retain state continuity through the massacre and industrial acceleration afterward.
- Retain formal families alongside later individual citizenship.
- Propose final ranks and institutional mechanics explicitly before treating them as accepted worldbuilding.
