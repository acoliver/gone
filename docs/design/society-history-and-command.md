# Society, history, and command

> **Status:** English companion digest of the crew civilization's history and command traditions. The Spanish bible at [docs/design/biblia.md](biblia.md) wins on story facts. The society handoff separates established direction from proposals and decisions still needed; this document preserves that boundary. See [docs/design/decisions.md](decisions.md) for open decisions.

## Reading the history

The comparisons to Earth below are shorthand for development discussion. They do not assign Earth dates, institutions, exact historical equivalents, or in-world names. The civilization has no kings in this account; “republic,” “empire,” “Romanization,” “Habsburg,” “Atatürk,” and “fascism” are explanatory comparisons only. No dates, durations, population totals, final rank names, or names for major historical figures are fixed. (society handoff, Purpose and authority; §§6–8)

## Established direction

### Environment and material development

Long-lasting volcanism, destructive waves, severe weather, and other disasters repeatedly decimated populations. The society that became the imperial center occupied the largest stable zone and an unusually dependable coast. Island chains made staged maritime expansion attractive. Stable farmland, harbors, resources, and supply routes supported expansion alongside ideological ambition. Later, volcanism and destructive waves abated, farmland increased, and difficult weather remained. The physical mechanism and geological timescale are unspecified. (society handoff §1)

Development did not follow Earth's timeline. Steam machinery appeared at the empire's beginning, encouraged by labor scarcity and mechanization incentives. The family and tribal system prevented formal slavery. Obstetrics and medicine developed early, including reliable late-agricultural birth control. Childbirth deaths were lower than deaths from major environmental hazards. No particular drug or mechanism is established. (society handoff §2) (society handoff §2)

Sexism existed, although women participated in economic work despite barriers. Women's military integration expanded from support roles to combat as the empire became overstretched; command integration came later. Fathers without sons sometimes preferred qualified daughters as command partners, opening a route into command without requiring egalitarian motives. (society handoff §2)

### Families, tribes, and households

Families carry durable economic, social, and relational obligations. Tribes began as geographic alliances of families, not castes, with informal class associations. Families changing the composition of an established tribe can be seen as betraying that community. A tribe changing an external alliance is generally political maneuvering, though an abandoned ally may call it betrayal. Migration does not automatically end membership. (society handoff §3)

There is no exact marriage institution. Property and household agreements and alliance partnerships can serve related functions. Arranged partnerships remain common but are becoming less fashionable among upper-class families or families whose position is secure. Polyamorous households have equal legal standing and a stronger lower-class association. (society handoff §4)

Homosexuality is not especially taboo; failure to have children draws more disapproval. A common “four” joins a lesbian couple and a gay male couple across classes, without implying every member desires every other member. “Four” later broadens into a term or gesture for gay or lesbian people; it is neutral in origin and can be used as an insult. Before women's military service, male same-sex relationships were more common in military settings, but did not necessarily constitute a modern household couple. The loose Greek comparison does not establish identical practices or romantic ideals. (society handoff §4)

Rules for parenthood, adoption, infertility, inheritance, leaving a household, and relationship exclusivity remain open. Earth comparisons here are shorthand, not a template for fictional law or custom. (society handoff §§4, 12)

### Eastern contact, communications, and political order

Regular contact with the eastern continent began relatively early. Eastern societies were broadly comparable in advancement, not primitive or isolated. Steam and telegraphic communication belong to the start of the empire. An eastern immigrant invented the telegraph and voice membrane to communicate with relatives. Imperial accounts glossed over his origin and motive without necessarily erasing him. Voice supports immediate conversation; coded messages remain useful for accuracy. The handoff fixes no engineering mechanism, route, bandwidth, or signaling alphabet. Eastern societies had not developed steam and communications in time to prevent the empire's early advantage. (society handoff §5) (society handoff §5)

There were never kings. Political authority developed through negotiated arrangements among tribes and influential families. “Republic” is an imperfect shorthand, not necessarily the people's name for the system. Expansion included conquest, alliances, negotiated incorporation, and willing accession. “Romanization” refers to forceful assimilation and loss or suppression of independent identities, not to a copied Roman system. (society handoff §6)

Power accumulated in one family. Its own tribe resented the concentration but tolerated the advantage; allied tribes resented subordination. The ruling society presented itself as nearly perfect, and leading families claimed a right and duty to govern and spread it. Material ambition and sincere belief coexisted. The handoff's “fascism” comparison does not import Earth's ideology, institutions, or sequence. (society handoff §6)

### Consolidation, massacre, and industrial change

World consolidation preceded the massacre, and the state survived it. Assimilation eroded the distinction between internal and external groups while economic stagnation increased grievances. A leading figure from a family and tribe that had migrated east built a coalition with eastern allies and formerly conquered groups. “Their Atatürk” is only a provisional comparison, not a name or borrowed biography. He praised imperial ancestors and condemned later descendants as corrupt and inbred. His movement was a restorationist power grab, not an original program for universal individual democracy. (society handoff §§7–8)

Pressure and embargo failed amid fragmented internal security, reduced perceived need for standing armies after external war ended, and unreliable cooperation among other families and tribes. This does not mean the government had no military capacity or that communications guaranteed obedience. (society handoff §7)

The violence was first celebrated as the “Great Reclaiming” and later reassessed as the “Great Holocaust” or massacre. These are working English labels; in-world names and official usage remain undecided. Violence extended beyond the ruling family to allies, collaborators, and associated people. The state continued and reformed. For a long time, the reformer was regarded as virtually messianic. Later memory recognizes that he was more brutal than the ruling family, alongside the continuity between imposing an ideal society and killing people blamed for corrupting it. Mechanisms, death toll, duration, target categories, and stages of reassessment are not established. (society handoff §8)

### Preserved chronology

1. Steam and communications appear at the empire's beginning. (society handoff §9)
2. Expansion and world consolidation occur across the imperial period. (society handoff §9)
3. Economic stagnation and concentrated political power help enable the eastern reformer's coalition. (society handoff §§7, 9)
4. The Great Reclaiming or massacre occurs as more intensive, standardized industrialization begins. Industrialization does not precede and cause the massacre. (society handoff §9)
5. The surviving state is initially reformed toward an idealized negotiated order from before the imperial concentration. (society handoff §9)
6. The Great Pax follows, with rapid industrial development and social change. (society handoff §9)
7. Individual democracy develops further while family institutions remain formally intact. (society handoff §§9–10)

The handoff compares the earlier material setting loosely to 1700s Britain and the emerging production scale to Henry Ford-style industrialization. Those comparisons describe uneven development and do not assign Earth dates or require Earth's technological sequence. Prosperity combines political continuity, existing technology, labor scarcity, lack of formal slavery, improving geology, and more farmland. It neither begins all industrial technology after the massacre nor justifies the atrocity. (society handoff §9)

### Democratization and continuing family institutions

Representation develops from negotiation among families and tribes toward family voting and eventually individual voting. Family institutions continue, but public office becomes separated from family possession: family obligations do not give a family the right to exercise an individual member's political rights. Informal family and tribal loyalties still shape politics and business; modern society is not free of patronage. The precise reforms and their sequence remain open. (society handoff §10)

## Dual command

### Established direction

Leadership traditions value redundancy under sustained high mortality. Byzantine duality is a loose inspiration, not an identical institution. Family members serving together is culturally less unusual than in many modern Earth forces. A dual was often a relative expected to cooperate and listen; daughters are a salient example. Romantic or household partners were possible but generally considered unwise. Daughter appointments helped women gain access to command. This history does not establish the captain's daughter as his dual, executive officer, or command holder. (society handoff §11)

“Commander” and “captain” are Earth placeholders. The language reference defines *nalu* as a command holder, not necessarily a military rank. Use [docs/design/language/nalesha-reference.md](language/nalesha-reference.md) for *nalu* and other existing terms. Do not coin Nalesha words. (society handoff §11; language reference, nalu entry)

### Unaccepted proposals

Earlier assistant proposals suggested separating personal grade, professional qualification, and appointed office; giving one dual-holder precedence in operations; defining succession and disagreement rules; and requiring independent qualifications for modern family command pairs. These remain proposals, not established direction. Suggested appointments and “First”/“Second” were not finalized. (society handoff §11)

The proposed English rank names **Learner, Member, Proven Member, Guide, Keeper, Senior Keeper, and High Keeper are rejected**. Do not use them as authoritative terminology. (society handoff §11; decisions brief, ruling 18)

### Decisions still needed

1. How equal are dual appointment holders, and what distinguishes a dual from an ordinary deputy?
2. Which offices require or permit dual holders?
3. How do disagreement, incapacity, succession, and removal work?
4. How did appointments change when families lost formal state authority?
5. Which qualifications, career grades, and offices exist in the modern service?
6. What are their English translations and eventual Nalesha terms?
7. What position, if any, does the captain's daughter hold? (society handoff §11)

## Unaccepted proposals and open historical decisions

No exact geological mechanism or timescale, medicine mechanism, communications engineering, historical names, massacre mechanism or death toll, or precise democratization sequence is established. Earth comparisons and suggested readings provide context only; they do not add fictional facts. (society handoff §§1–2, 4–10, 12–13)
