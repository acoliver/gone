# Dry-aged brass and a closed-slot suspender, issue #130

This pass addresses the in-game review at PR head `a23b4f6`. The leaf measures captures and does not judge images. Image approval remains with the driver. The comparison sheets put `a23b4f6` on the left and this pass on the right, with separate comparisons against `ef57c29`.

## Causes, evidence and changes

**Exposed brass had no aged film.** Outside the cavity and convex-edge masks, the former Present atlas had mean linear RGB `(0.669007, 0.436218, 0.148174)`, effectively identical to Remembered `(0.669012, 0.436221, 0.148176)`. Its median roughness was 0.384314 and metallic 0.960784. The cavity mask could therefore pass the old tarnish-correlation gate while leaving exposed brass unchanged. `medal_dry_finish.py` now gives exposed Present brass a thin, darker, less saturated oxide film, with brighter convex edges and deeper brown-black recesses. The film recipe retains exposed median roughness about 0.533333 and metallic 0.890196. A separate brass-only reflectance calibration, described below, scales its RGB before export. The exposed surface remains mostly conducting; opaque low-metallic oxide remains concentrated in cavities. There is no green growth, wet streak or uniform dirt overlay.

**The former highlights clipped.** The stage-three game masks measured an RGB channel at or above 0.99 on 24.6483% of Remembered and 32.0561% of Present rim pixels. At the fixed medal camera, the diagnostic light inventory recorded four ceiling lights with energy 3.2 and specular 0.5, at distances 2.4311–6.1465 m. These are engine settings and distances, not calibrated incident lux. The former exposed Remembered roughness had median 0.301961; its bright conductor colour and narrow GGX lobes concentrated the direct highlights, with negligible measured room reflection between them. An initial roughness increase to median 0.392157 broadened the saturated band and raised Remembered clipping to 28.4835%. A fixed-camera material grid isolated roughness from reflectance; increasing roughness alone did not solve that state. The delivered Remembered map narrows the polished lobe by multiplying that roughness field by 0.6, retains metallic 0.984314, and scales brass reflectance by 0.13. Present uses a 0.15 reflectance calibration after its oxide film. These factors are applied to the editable atlas, not to exposure or a painted highlight. They are a fixed-rig appearance calibration, not measured optical constants for brass. Final quantized exposed values are: Remembered linear RGB `(0.066119, 0.043088, 0.014498)`, median roughness `0.235294`, median metallic `0.984314`; Present RGB `(0.044934, 0.031923, 0.013699)`, roughness `0.533333`, metallic `0.890196`. Enamel keeps its geometry and blue colour; its coat is 0.35 instead of 0.65, with coat roughness 0.18/0.26 and a 0.035 base-roughness increase. Medal specular remains 0.5. No light, camera, tonemap or global environment setting changed.

**The room probe was ineffective at the medal.** The original room probe covered the medal geometrically, but captured from the room centre, outside the enclosing wardrobe, and used cull mask 16 while light instances remain on layer 1. Receiver masks and instance layers serve different purposes. Native-reflection diagnostics also isolated the probe ambient-mode setting on the installed Metal renderer. `QuartersMedalReflection` adds one native, box-projected probe per wardrobe state, bounded to the carcass at 0.650002 × 2.200001 × 1.0 m. Its capture origin is 50 mm inside the front plane, looking into the existing room through the open wardrobe. Capture mask 17 includes the existing light instances and quarters surfaces; reflection mask 16 leaves receiver layers unchanged. It inherits the existing disabled, black environment ambient rather than injecting ambient colour. Only the local probes refresh during the existing power fade, then return to one-shot updates. Existing room probes and all light definitions remain unchanged. Actual game controls separately disable the local probe, all quarters probes, the light capture layer, and the local probe's ambient contribution mode.

**Ribbon and ring competed for the same bar.** The former 0.8 mm ribbon could not fit the 0.130 mm bar/claw gap with 0.080 mm clearance on each side. That caused the split-tab compromise. `medal_suspender.py` replaces only the bar with a solid closed-slot frame and lower integral ring lug. The ribbon passes through a 23 × 1.6 mm slot and folds full-width around the rail; the ring uses the lower lug. The body, integral eyelet and jump ring retain their accepted vertices, topology, UVs, material regions and smooth flags. All upper sewn samples and RibbonBar remain exact.

The ribbon now has a separate material derived from its existing stripe colours, with periodic weave albedo, tangent normal and roughness tiles, filtered minification, mild fibre sheen and restrained Present fading. Its source material uses a current Blender Mix node: the older MixRGB node did not let this Blender glTF exporter recognize the vertex-colour multiplier and would have exported white colours. Exact exported colour assertions caught that defect. Normalized integer colour accessors are decoded according to glTF before comparison; no colour tolerance was relaxed. The glTF sampler transform comes from the source weave mapping and is retained by the runtime adapter.

## Geometry and preservation

The slot frame and full-width loop add 124 triangles relative to `a23b4f6`: 40 metal and 84 ribbon. Totals are 21,962 Remembered and 28,499 Present, below the unchanged 22,943/28,728 ceilings and 1.5 aged/clean ratio. The solid medal is 8,310 triangles; ribbon is 740. All four components are closed, consistently wound and positive-volume. Strict self/metal/cloth crossing counts are zero. Exported minimum metal/ribbon separation is 0.099124/0.099133 mm, above the unchanged 0.080 mm requirement. Continuous metal/wool clearance remains 0.250999/0.251001 mm. Reverse support remains 96 anchors, with maximum metal support distance 1.969605/0.920816 mm. Body depths remain 3.973170/2.180004 mm, excluding the claw.

The independent accepted-metal verifier confirms exact retained topology, corner UVs, material regions and smooth flags, with position storage differences at most 0.0000201 mm. Source and export verifiers freeze every non-Medal part, including Buttons, Placket and RibbonBar. Unrelated vertices, normals, UVs, colours, transforms, materials and embedded textures are exact. Contact was rebaked from the final mounted assembly, with the original 3 mm bound. Its ratio remains exactly one outside that bound. The full-width suspension exposes additional cloth: the Remembered measured crescent grew from 26,973 to 32,906 pixels, including 10,639 newly sampled pixels with zero median contact reduction. In the common 22,267-pixel region, median reduction remained 7.9917%; the changed population, rather than weakened old contact, lowered the new whole-crescent median to 4.8997%. Remembered bounded direct-light influence is therefore 0.85 instead of 0.75; Present remains 0.35. The 5–20% visibility gate, 3 mm locality bound and all support/clearance checks remain unchanged.

Age-off controls apply the same highlight calibration as production, remove only the brass oxide/staining and leave enamel unchanged. This prevents the highlight correction itself from being counted as visible tarnish.

**Fine normals needed filtering for the sharper polish.** The unchanged micro-finish map, paired with Remembered roughness 0.235294, produced 6,815 isolated temporal spike samples in the 24-frame arc, compared with 43,108 in the overstrong unfiltered control. The signal is the full-minus-geometric-normal difference, so the changing highlight alone is subtracted. A controlled fine-only three-texel Gaussian filter reduced the diagnostic count to 204, all in one initial transient frame; its normalized temporal p99.9 fell from 0.156852 to 0.055340. Production filters only the Remembered brass fine-normal delta against the retained geometric bake, preserves enamel samples, and reads the frozen normal source on every run to avoid cumulative smoothing. No geometric bevel is blurred. The final warmed production sweep is reported below. Two regression tests establish preservation of geometric variation and unit normals, and suppression of subpixel checker detail; their first run failed because the filter was not implemented.
